# Every assignment gets a day, not just today — 2026-08-27

## The ask

"make the scheduler better it should allocate time to every homework after estimating
completing it and other events and stuff"

## What is already true, so it does not get rebuilt

Estimation is done. `coursework.py` reads real numbers off the assignments — 172 open
items carry `estimate_source = 'analyzed'` for 121 hours of work, and `analyzed` is also
what makes a candidate `divisible`, so a 344-minute milestone already arrives as sittings
rather than as one unschedulable lump. `docs/04 §1.3` forbids auto-retuning the type
defaults, and nothing here touches them.

The single-day planner is also fine at what it does: P1–P20 hold, `select` refuses work it
cannot place, coursework already gets first claim on `homework_target_minutes`.

## The actual gap

**The planner has a horizon of one day.** `candidates()` sorts everything not due this
week into `PRIORITY_REST` and `select` spends today's budget; whatever is left is
"overflow", counted and forgotten. Today's real plan said *216 item(s) did not fit* — a
number that names no day, no assignment and no consequence. An assignment due 15 September
is invisible every morning until the week it is due, and then it competes with everything
else due that week for a Thursday with 145 free minutes.

So: 255 hours of open work, no statement anywhere about when any of it happens, and no
warning that some of it cannot happen at all.

## The fix

A forward allocation over a horizon, recomputed on demand, that answers two questions the
system currently cannot: *which day is this assignment actually getting?* and *what will
not finish before it is due?*

**A forecast, not a plan.** No `day_plan` rows for future days — those would fight
`proposed`/`accepted`/`superseded`, the `--if-missing` catch-up, and docs/04 §6's
"proposed, never imposed". **Nothing is stored either.** A table would need a migration, a
staleness story and a fingerprint to keep rule 3 (two runs, no upstream change, zero
writes); recomputing ~14 days of capacity is a handful of reads and has none of that.

**Earliest deadline first, packed as early as it fits.** Frontloading rather than
just-in-time, because it agrees with the owner's own two-hours-of-homework-a-weekday
ruling and because slack in front of a deadline is the whole point of planning it.

## Steps

1. `plan/runway.py` — `allocate(conn, settings, start, horizon)`. Walk the days forward;
   per-day capacity through `capacity.compute`, which already resolves the day's own
   timezone (the owner moves between UTC-7 and UTC+5:30, so a horizon that assumed one
   zone would drift). Skip a day under the P3 floor. Then EDF: for each dated candidate,
   in due order, lay `sitting()`-sized pieces onto the earliest days that have room.
   It must mirror `select`'s placement rule — indivisible work larger than a day's
   largest slot does not go on that day — or the forecast promises days the planner will
   refuse, which is worse than no forecast.

2. `planner.candidates()` — take the ids the runway put on *this* day, the same way it
   already takes `at_risk_goals`, and give them their own priority band. Above
   `DUE_THIS_WEEK` (the runway has already weighed the deadline, and a thing it says to
   start today needs to start today) and below `DUE_TODAY` and `AT_RISK_GOAL`, which are
   statements the runway does not make.

3. `planner.propose()` — call the runway, pass the ids through, and replace the useless
   half of the overflow note. "216 did not fit" says nothing; **"N assignments cannot
   finish before they are due"**, named, is the sentence worth waking up to.

4. Tests: EDF order; a piece of work that cannot fit before its deadline is reported and
   not silently dropped; rule 3 idempotency; and the 2026-08-24 scar as a regression —
   a CIS 236 RFP due 13 November must not take today's first block while seven
   assignments due 28 August are uncovered. EDF should make that unrepresentable.

## Decisions taken, so they are not re-argued

- **Horizon is 14 days, as a setting rather than a constant.** Past the Apple Calendar
  sync window the only fixed events on a future day are the `calendar:asu` classes, so
  capacity out there reads optimistically. Fourteen days is far enough to see a fortnight
  of coursework and near enough that the capacity behind it is real.
- **The allocation is advice to the day planner, not a second planner.** `propose` stays
  the only thing that writes a day.

## Not in scope

A runway page or panel. The deliverable is that the daily plan now contains the work that
has to start today and warns about what will not finish — which shows up in the brief, the
dashboard and the schedule without a new surface.

## What the live ledger changed, after the plan was written

Three things, all found by running against `data/backglass.db` rather than a fixture.

1. **Overdue work was allocated nothing.** The cutoff `day > deadline` is true for every
   day of a horizon that starts after the deadline, so the five most urgent obligations on
   the board got zero minutes and were then reported as impossible. Late work has no
   deadline left to beat; the only useful answer is "as early as there is room".
2. **Everything due past day 14 was called impossible.** The fortnight had stopped before
   the deadline — the work had not failed to fit. A November exam declared impossible in
   August is a false alarm, and false alarms are how the true ones get ignored.
3. **The plan contradicted itself.** The first live run warned that a quiz would not finish
   in time, nine lines under the 7:00am block scheduling it, because the runway and
   `select` are not the same algorithm. `select` writes the day, so `select` wins: the
   warning is now computed after selection and never names work the day just covered.

Also shipped, because "allocate time to every homework" is not much use if nothing shows
the allocation: `backglass plan --runway` prints one line per obligation in deadline
order, with the days and minutes it was given. Behind a flag — the plan is a statement
about today, and fourteen days of table under it every morning would be the longest thing
on the screen.

## Measured after

220 candidates, 155 allocated across the fortnight, 14 on the first day, 3 that genuinely
cannot finish before their deadline. A plan run takes ~5s. The duplicates showing up in
the unreachable list ("Complete AI Scholar Welcome Survey" twice, "Complete the Welcome
Survey") are a ledger dedup matter, not a runway one — `backglass duplicates`.

## The panel, added after the CLI

The runway now also sits on the Schedule page, as a fold under the day — `_runway.html`,
served by `GET /schedule/runway`. Rows in deadline order; what will not fit leads the
list, because it is the only part that asks for a decision.

Two things about it are deliberate:

- **It loads on first open, never with the page.** `runway.allocate` walks fourteen days
  of capacity and measures 0.58s on the live ledger. The Schedule page is opened every
  morning and must not pay that to show today, so the fold carries
  `hx-trigger="toggle once"` and the day page is exactly as fast as it was.
- **The chip says "Won't fit", not "Overdue".** The first draft reused the `overdue`
  chip and produced a chip that lied: work due *today* that the fortnight has nowhere to
  put is not late, it is about to be. Same alarm and same ink, a different sentence,
  because the owner does something different about each — a second label on k-verm, the
  way `due` is already a second label on k-black.

Verified on the live ledger through the browser: 168 rows, 12 of them flagged, every
other obligation carrying its days and minutes.


## The half of the board that was invisible (same day, second pass)

Owner: *"look into pipeline to see why more assignments and things in the runway aren't
being added."*

The pipeline is not the problem. 206 Canvas assignments are in the ledger and 204 of them
carry an open commitment; the two that do not were extracted correctly and then dropped by
hand. The funnel, measured on the live ledger:

| | |
|---|---|
| open commitments | 449 |
| `owed_to_me` — someone else's work, never planned | 69 |
| below `confidence_threshold` 0.7 — the review queue, rule 2 | 102 (13 of them assignments) |
| stale-gated, waiting on an answer at /ask | 11 |
| **into `runway.allocate`** | **267** |
| came out with a day | 137 |
| came out flagged as unreachable | 1 |
| **came out with nothing at all** | **130** (89 of them assignments) |

The last row is the answer. The pool needs 12,100 minutes; the fourteen days in front of
it hold 5,525. Work whose deadline is past `horizon_end` cannot be called unreachable —
that guard exists so a November exam is not reported impossible in August — so an
obligation that got no minutes *and* is due after the fortnight matched neither branch and
left no trace on any surface. The runway read as the whole board while it was the half of
it that fitted.

Fixed by stating it. `Runway.beyond` carries `Unplaced(item, minutes)` for exactly that
case — dated, unfinished, due past the horizon — and both surfaces print one sentence
from it: *"97h across 111 obligation(s) has no day in the next 14 — due after they end,
and they have no minutes left."* Counted rather than listed, the shape `_echo_overflow`
already settled on. Undated work is deliberately not in it: "due after the fortnight" is a
false statement about something with no date.

Not fixed by extending the horizon. Fourteen days is a decision with reasons in the
module docstring, and the capacity out past the calendar sync window is already optimistic.

## The Now line moves

Owner, same session: *"make the time move in real time."* `_now_top` ran once, on the
server, when the page was built — so a schedule opened at 8am still marked 8am at one in
the afternoon. `static/now.js` re-runs that arithmetic in the browser every 15s from data
attributes the canvas carries (window in minutes, pixels per minute, day, timezone), on
both the day canvas and the week grid. The timezone is `view.tz`, not the browser's,
because the owner moves between UTC-7 and UTC+5:30. The server now draws the line on every
view of today and hides it when the clock is outside the drawn window; the script only
moves and reveals it, so an htmx swap of `#panel-timeline` cannot leave a dead listener
behind.

## Canvas enrichment — what the feed cannot say (2026-08-27, third pass)

Owner: *"continue to enrich processor."* Chosen target: enrich the `assignment` rows from
Canvas.

**The premise was wrong and the measurement is why it did not ship that way.** The plan
was to pull assignment descriptions off Canvas's REST API, because 85 of the ledger's 206
assignments carry no description and 134 fall back to the type table. Checked first,
against 205 assignments matched to a live export:

- the API's description was longer on **zero** of them — 164 byte-identical, 41 *shorter*,
  because `canvas_ics` keeps the link URLs the API's HTML hides behind anchor text;
- every assignment with no description in the ledger has none in Canvas either;
- re-running `coursework.effort_for` over the API text changed **0 of 205** estimates and
  0 of 8,221 minutes.

The ICS feed is not thin. It was never the problem, and `tasks/todo.md`'s D1 decline
(2026-08-25) was right for a second reason it did not know about.

What the API does know, and `connectors/canvas_ics.py` predicted in its own docstring:
**submission state.** Thirteen assignments Canvas had already graded were still open
commitments holding 434 minutes of planner capacity, one of them on that morning's plan.

Built: migration 0035 (`points_possible`, `submitted_at`, `submission_state`, `score`,
`enriched_at` on `assignment`), `backglass/canvas_enrich.py`, and
`backglass coursework --enrich <file> [--close-submitted] [--dry-run]`. The export snippet
lives in docs/07 §Canvas.

Three things are load-bearing:

- **An import, not a connector.** ASU disables student tokens; that is why `canvas_ics`
  exists, and its docstring forswears session cookies. So the owner exports the document
  from their own signed-in tab and the program reads a file. Nothing here fetches.
- **Separate columns, not `description`.** `coursework.upsert` rewrites `description` and
  every effort column on every sync, and launchd runs it every thirty minutes — an
  enrichment written into a column the upsert owns would evaporate within the hour with no
  error. A test asserts a feed re-sync leaves the enrichment standing.
- **`graded` does not mean handed in.** Canvas writes a graded zero for work that never
  arrived. A row counts as finished only with a `submitted_at`, an explicitly submitted
  state, or a mark above zero. The live export carried two graded zeros with no
  submission — one for an assignment still three days from its due date — and the first
  draft of the rule would have closed both.

Applied to the live ledger: 205 rows enriched, 116 export rows unmatched (undated Canvas
items the dated feed never carried, counted and never inserted), second pass 0 writes.
With owner sign-off, 11 commitments closed, each with the Canvas evidence in
`resolution_note`.
